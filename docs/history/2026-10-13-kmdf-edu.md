## A KMDF Plug and Play driver on real resources

The last part of step 30.4 of the Windows driver model (roadmap Phase 30,
[design/windows-drivers.md](design/windows-drivers.md)): a KMDF driver
for QEMU's `edu` PCI device runs on `Wdf01000.sys` with the device's
memory, interrupt and DMA, and passes the checks the WDM `edu` driver
(step 30.3) passes.

- **`kmdfedu.sys`** (`tools/drivertest/kmdfedu.c`, `kmdfedu.inf`): written
  as the WDK's PCI samples are.  `EvtDriverDeviceAdd` creates the device,
  its interface, an interrupt object and a queue at `PASSIVE_LEVEL`;
  `EvtDevicePrepareHardware` maps the memory BAR from the translated
  resources and creates two DMA enablers (scatter/gather and packet, with
  `AddressWidthOverride` 28 for edu's DMA mask); `EvtDeviceQueryRemove`
  refuses removal while a handle is open.  The ISR acknowledges the device
  and queues the interrupt's DPC.  A DMA request sends the buffer to the
  device in a scatter/gather transaction, takes it back into a common
  buffer (`WdfCommonBufferCreate`) and again through a packet transaction
  (map registers).  It answers the same control codes as `edutest.sys`, so
  `edutest.exe` drives either; the driver now says in its answer whether it
  is the KMDF one, and `edutest` then checks what `WdfInterruptGetInfo`
  reports instead of the WDM connect details.
- **DMA version 3** (`kernel/nt/dma.c`): KMDF runs its DMA transactions
  through the version 3 adapter calls when the adapter is version 3, which
  NovaOS's had only declared.  They are in now: `GetDmaTransferInfo`,
  `InitializeDmaTransferContext`, `GetScatterGatherListEx` and
  `BuildScatterGatherListEx`, `AllocateAdapterChannelEx`, `MapTransferEx`
  (several elements when the device reaches the pages, else the map
  registers' bounce buffer), `FlushAdapterBuffersEx`,
  `CancelAdapterChannel` and `FreeAdapterObject`, on the same lists and
  bounce buffers as the version 1 calls.
- **The build** (`tools/kmdf/build_kmdf.py`): links `kmdfedu.sys` with the
  KMDF client stub as it does `kmdftest.sys`, and makes its package, signed
  by a test-signed catalog only; `build_userland.py` puts it in
  `C:\Tests\KmdfEdu`.
- **The test** (`tests/selftest/devices/kmdfedu`, the devices suite's
  "kmdfedu" boot): with test signing on, `pnputil /add-driver kmdfedu.inf
  /install` installs and starts it; `edutest` computes a factorial through
  the interrupt and moves 4096 and 1500 bytes by DMA; removal is vetoed
  while it is open; `pnputil` disables it (the device is deleted and the
  driver unloads) and enables it; after `edutest msi 1` and a restart of
  the device the framework connects the interrupt object to the MSI
  message, and after a restart of the machine the driver starts at boot.
